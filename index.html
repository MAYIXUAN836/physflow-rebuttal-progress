Thank you for highlighting PhysicEdit. We appreciate its use of video-derived transition priors to guide physical image editing without rendering intermediate videos at inference. We agree that our original discussion should have made this connection explicit, and we have expanded the related-work discussion accordingly.

Our contribution is the progressive internalization of textual and visual process supervision into a continuous latent plan. PhysicEdit combines an explicit textual reasoning trace at inference with video-supervised transition queries. PhysFlow progressively compresses high-level physical rationales, low-level transition descriptions, and ordered intermediate visual blocks into six latent tokens. These tokens condition the image generator together with a separate 16-token final visual block. This design internalizes the textual reasoning as well as the visual process, so inference uses the compact latent plan without decoding a textual reasoning trace. We clarify this distinction in Sections 2 and 3. PhysicEdit, Section 3.3.

62MG / xuAm · 相对 EndoCoT、Monet 的贡献

PhysFlow learns a compact plan from supervision of both the physical transformation and its outcome. A target image specifies the desired result; physical rationales and intermediate visual states provide additional supervision about the transformation. Our contribution is a training curriculum that compresses these complementary signals into six latent tokens while retaining a separate 16-token final visual block. This allocates different representation budgets to the transformation and its endpoint, and gives the image generator access to both (Sections 3.3 and 3.4).

Relative to EndoCoT, we change the representation and its supervision during compression. EndoCoT establishes a closely related approach with recurrent thought states, intermediate image-generation supervision, and terminal textual grounding. Its terminal-consolidation stage retains recurrent thought updates while stopping gradient propagation through intermediate steps. It also avoids decoding intermediate images at inference, a property we share. EndoCoT, Section 4.2. PhysFlow replaces high-level physical rationales, low-level transition descriptions, and then successive intermediate visual blocks with individual latent tokens. Each visual replacement removes that block's direct alignment loss; later uncompressed blocks, final-block alignment, and the flow-matching loss continue to train the compressed tokens. The curriculum therefore changes how the planner represents the process and receives supervision as it approaches the inference representation. At inference, the generator receives all six plan tokens together with the separate final visual block (Sections 3.3 and 3.4).

Relative to Monet, we jointly internalize textual and visual process supervision for image generation. Monet distills auxiliary visual information into latent embeddings within text-latent interleaved reasoning and further trains them with visual-latent policy optimization. Its latent embeddings support a model that produces textual answers. Monet, Section 3. PhysFlow compresses both the textual rationales and the intermediate visual representations into a continuous plan before generating the edited image. During planner training, the flow-matching loss propagates through a frozen image generator to train the conditioning representations. We then freeze the planner and adapt the generator to these representations. This couples learning the plan to its use in image generation (Section 3.4).

The same-data control now measures the gain beyond adaptation to the training pairs. PhysFlow scores 56.32 on PICABench-Superficial, compared with 55.12 for image-pair SFT on the same PhysTrace pairs. Both use the same generator LoRA configuration and 537 generator updates; PhysFlow additionally trains its planner, for 1,790 total updates. The terminal-only run scores 55.57, compared with 56.32 for full PhysFlow as reported in the paper. The 0.75-point difference compares training configurations; finer attribution to supervision and curriculum requires further controls. These comparisons report single-run point estimates. We clarify these design differences in Sections 2 and 3 and report each completed control alongside the claim it tests.
